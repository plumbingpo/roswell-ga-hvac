Two situations call for quick action from you before a technician arrives. The first is an iced-over air conditioner: turn the system off at the thermostat and set the fan to "on" so the moving air thaws the coil, because running it frozen can overflow the drain pan and damage the compressor. The second is a furnace that keeps cycling on and off or produces a burning smell. Shut it down at the switch and call us. That small step can save you from a far bigger repair.

How Macon Homeowners Can Extend HVAC Life

Routine maintenance is the difference between a system that lasts and one that quits in the middle of a heat wave. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: fresh filters every one to three months, two feet of clearance around the outdoor condenser, a gentle rinse each spring for the condenser coil, and two professional tune-ups a year, so problems are found while they're still inexpensive.

Age matters too. Most central air conditioners run 12 to 15 years, furnaces 15 to 20, and heat pumps 10 to 15 with regular service. If your system is past that window and starting to struggle, such as rising energy bills, rooms that never quite reach temperature, or repairs stacking up season after season, a planned replacement is nearly always less expensive and far less stressful than an emergency one during the first heat wave of summer.

Small problems rarely stay small when they're ignored. Take a weak capacitor: swapping it out is a quick, inexpensive repair, but if it's ignored for a season, it can burn out the compressor, and suddenly a minor fix becomes a full system replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to keep our number in their phone and call at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and prevents a miserable night.
